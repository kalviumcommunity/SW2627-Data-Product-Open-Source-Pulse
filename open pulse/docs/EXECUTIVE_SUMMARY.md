# Churn Reduction Initiative

## Executive Summary

*One page. Prepared for the leadership team. The supporting analysis is in
[TECHNICAL_ANALYSIS.md](TECHNICAL_ANALYSIS.md) and is optional reading.*

---

### Situation

We lose $778K a year to customer churn, 6.4% of a $12.1M customer book. That loss is not spread evenly, and over half of it is avoidable. We examined all 1,000 customers to find where it sits and what would stop it.

### Key findings

- **SMB is the leak.** It loses 13.1% of its customers a year against 3.0% in Enterprise - $328K a year, 78% of everything recoverable.
- **We already know how to fix it.** Enterprise runs at 3.0% inside this same business. Bringing our three weaker segments to that standard returns $420K a year.
- **The cause we assumed was wrong.** Support contact does not drive customers away. Length of relationship does: our newest customers leave at 15.6%, our longest-standing at 10.7%.

### Business risks

- **Concentrated revenue leak.** Left alone, SMB churn costs us $985K over three years. It is 78% of everything we could recover, and the gap widens every quarter we do not act.
- **Largest accounts carry the largest exposure.** Enterprise has our best retention at 3.0%, yet $227K is at risk each year. Each account is worth $145K. Losing two costs more than fixing our worst-rate segment entirely.
- **We are about to act on a false signal.** An earlier read of a small sample suggested support contact drives customers away. Across the full base it does not. A campaign to reduce support contact would have quietened our most valuable accounts and changed churn by nothing.

### Recommendations

- **Fund a retention programme for SMB.** Worth $328K a year, the single largest prize. Owner: Head of Customer Success.
- **Give every Enterprise account a named owner.** $227K sits in 53 accounts worth $145K each. Owner: Head of Sales.
- **Rebuild the first 6 months of the customer relationship,** where customers leave at 15.6% against 10.7% later on. Owner: Head of Customer Success.

### Decision needed

Approve scoping of a retention programme for SMB and confirm a budget ceiling. Any programme costing less than $420K a year to run pays for itself, because that is the full annual prize. We do not yet hold cost data; Finance must price the options.

### Next steps

- Customer Success returns three costed options for the SMB programme - two weeks.
- Finance prices them against the ceiling above - three weeks.
- Sales assigns owners to all 53 Enterprise accounts - this quarter.
- Support begins recording response times so the question we could not answer becomes answerable - this quarter.
